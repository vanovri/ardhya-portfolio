01 / Problem

Problem

Our team built a rehabilitation-hand system for an undergraduate thesis. It needed a dependable way to acquire surface EMG (the electrical activity of muscles), turn it into stable features, and show the whole system's state to an operator.

My part was the EMG acquisition and signal-processing subsystem, plus the full monitoring interface. Aldin focused on the stimulator and Zain on fuzzy-control design.

02 / Approach

Approach

SEN0240sEMG sensor, analog 0–3.0 V
STM32 ADC12-bit, timer-triggered DMA, nominal 4 kHz
Digital filters20 Hz HPF · 500 Hz LPF · 50 Hz notch
FeaturesRMS & MAV over 400 samples (~100 ms)
UART → GUI115200 baud · Python/Tkinter · CSV

Firmware processes ADC samples in half/full DMA buffers, filters them and computes RMS/MAV per window, then streams telemetry over UART. The Tkinter GUI parses the serial stream, plots EMG features, monitors status and logs to CSV. Coding agents assisted the implementation, and I reviewed and tested what they produced.

05 / Lessons

Lessons

  • Breaking a sensor-to-screen problem into acquisition, processing, transport and display made each part testable.
  • Mains interference at 50 Hz is visible in the spectrum, so the notch filter earns its place.
  • Program configuration and measured results are separate claims, and I keep them separate.
  • AI assistance is useful when I read and check what it produces. I disclose it.
